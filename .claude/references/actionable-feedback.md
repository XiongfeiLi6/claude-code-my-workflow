# Actionable academic feedback

Use when reviewing a manuscript, proposal, analysis or teaching artifact; scale
detail to the requested review. This guidance does not launch additional reviewers.

- Establish the artifact's purpose, stage and requested review scope. Check that
  relevant sections/pages were actually read before alleging missing content.
- Draft anchored comments first, then synthesize the assessment around the most
  consequential issues. Do not manufacture concerns to meet a numerical quota.
- For each major finding give an exact location, source evidence, consequence for
  the stated objective, and a feasible repair or the evidence needed to choose one.
  If no feasible repair is known, say so; do not invent an easy solution.
- Distinguish required corrections, unresolved questions and optional improvements.
  For costly requests, state the likely benefit and effort qualitatively; prefer
  a smaller diagnostic when it can answer the same question. Preserve source-based
  minority concerns rather than treating reviewer agreement as proof.
- Before a proposed diagnostic runs, state what its plausible outcomes would and
  would not establish. A repair proposal does not authorize changing an estimand,
  sample, inference procedure or approved wording.
- Keep proposed checks separate from checks performed. Report unreadable material,
  unavailable data and other limits. Do not replace the user's shared style with
  a reviewer's voice or preferences.

For structured review pipelines, keep the existing findings schema unchanged;
put these explanations in its existing evidence/suggestion fields or accompanying
prose. See the [source review](../../docs/ACADEMIC_WORKFLOW_REVIEW.md).
