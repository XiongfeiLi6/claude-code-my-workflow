# Teaching objectives and evidence

Read for pedagogical review or exercise design, not for unrelated research tasks.

- Use the instructor's learning objectives, audience/prerequisites, time budget
  and rubric. If these are unavailable, state assumptions; offer provisional
  feedback without inventing institutional policy, grades or rubric weights.
- Where AI use matters, distinguish what learners must demonstrate unaided from
  what the instructor intends them to learn through AI-assisted work. Treat an
  unsettled distinction as a question for the instructor, not a prohibition.
- Anchor feedback to a slide, exercise or response and the relevant objective or
  rubric criterion. Identify the highest-value feasible improvement per affected
  criterion; do not turn every preference into a defect.
- Check for an opportunity to demonstrate understanding, such as explaining an
  assumption, interpreting a result or transferring the idea to a new example.
  A polished deck or answer alone does not establish learning.
- Recommend rehearsal of generated presentations against the actual time budget.
  Distinguish source-based pacing estimates from an observed rehearsal; preserve
  the instructor's presentation style and format.
- Student work and identifiers follow project privacy rules. Do not request or
  expose identifiable student records just to illustrate feedback.
